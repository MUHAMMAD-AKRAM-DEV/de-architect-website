The space

Built for
drawing in.

We took a floor of a working foundry building in 2014 and did to it exactly what we tell clients to do: kept the good bones, opened it up, and put the light where the work is.

A day here

Nine to six,
roughly.

  1. 08:30

    The coffee and the wall

    Whoever is in first puts the machine on and looks at the drawing wall. Anything pinned up overnight gets argued about before the day starts properly.

  2. 09:30

    Desk work

    The quiet stretch. Plans, sections, details. The rule is no meetings before eleven, and it mostly survives contact with reality.

  3. 11:00

    Site or client

    Someone is always out — measuring a house, walking a site with a contractor, or sitting at the long table with a client and a model.

  4. 14:00

    The model shop

    Cutting, gluing, and the smell of MDF. Every project gets built at least once in card before it gets built in concrete.

  5. 16:30

    Pin-up

    Work goes on the wall and gets picked apart by everyone, including the person who joined last month. Nothing leaves the studio without this.

  6. 18:00

    Lights off

    We do not do heroic overtime. Tired people draw badly and miss things, and someone has to pay for that later.

The kit

Sharp pencils,
good software.

Neither one on its own. We sketch by hand because it is faster to think that way, and we model everything because it is the only way to be sure it fits together.

Working here
