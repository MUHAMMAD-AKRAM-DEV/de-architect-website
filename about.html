Our story

Sixteen years of
starting with the brief.

The studio began in 2009 with one house and a conviction that has not changed since: a building should be shaped by the life inside it, not the other way round. That first client wanted somewhere to cook, read and have people over — so we designed around a kitchen and let the rest follow.

We have grown since, into interiors, workspaces and restoration, but the method is the same. We spend the first weeks listening, measuring, and watching where the light lands. The drawing comes after.

Today the practice is fourteen people across two studios, working on everything from a single reimagined room to a whole building brought back into use. No project is too small if the thinking is worth doing.

How we work

Four stages,
and no surprises.

Every project runs the same way. You always know which stage you are in, what is being decided, and what it costs before it starts.

  1. 01

    Listen

    We visit, measure and talk — about routine, not style. What time you eat, where you work, which room nobody uses. The brief comes out of that conversation.

  2. 02

    Draw

    Sketches, plans and a physical model. You see options in three dimensions before anything is fixed, and we expect to throw the first one away.

  3. 03

    Resolve

    Materials, joinery, light and detail, plus the permissions and costings. This is the longest stage and the one that decides whether the building works.

  4. 04

    Build

    We stay on site until the keys change hands. The people who drew it are the people who see it finished.

What we believe

Light before finish

Where the sun falls decides a plan. Materials are chosen afterwards, to suit the light a room actually gets.

Build less, better

The cheapest and greenest square metre is the one you do not build. We would rather rework what exists than add to it.

Detail is not decoration

A junction that is thought through disappears. Most of what we draw is meant never to be noticed.

One team throughout

No handover to a delivery office. The people at the first meeting are on site at the last.
